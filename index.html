Warship Compatibility Quiz

Which Cold War-era warship is your soul mate?

Have you ever wondered which finely-crafted naval weapon of war is your soul mate? No? Pffft, of course you have, don't be ridiculous.

Anyway, now you don't have to! Merely answer these objective and totally-not-loaded questions and let's discover which floating brick has the key to your heart!

After x amount of seconds, our top scientists from our best free-range asylums have found the warship that is meant for you! Are you ready?

USS Nimitz!

A Nimitz-class aircraft carrier. Displacing over 100,000 tons and over 1,000 feet long, the USS Nimitz is an absolute unit! She might never walk down the catwalk at New York Fashion Week, but she has all the space you need to start your comically oversized family, with plenty of hangar space to spare. While not the type of ship to end a fight, her massive amount of "kids" makes her presence known to all around her,even your sworn enemies. Built in 1975, she's still going strong today, and her speed of 32 knots makes her fast for her impressive size.

TAVKR Kiev!

The first Kiev-class aircraft carrier, although she'll tell you she's a "large aviation cruiser" (sure, whatever). Equipped with helicopters and light aircraft, she's great at digging up the tea on others, and she has plenty of ways to defend herself if that gets her into trouble. Built in 1975, she's a young and energetic gal, even if her modest aircraft means she won't easily engage in a fight. She can hold up to 30 aircraft, making her ideal to start your own little traditional Russian family. At 45,000 tons and 900 feet long, she's perfect for someone who wants a little more to hold onto, and her speed of 32 knots means she can both get shit done while also enjoying the little things in life.

USS Forrestal!

A Forrestal-class aircraft carrier who, being built in 1955, has all the age and experience you could ever want in a partner. Weighing over 60,000 tons and over 1,000 feet long, there's plenty of her to go around. At 33 knots, she's a speedy gal, and her huge "hangars" are perfect for all your personal and familial needs. Her large aircraft complement means she'll love to start a fight, even if she can't necessarily finish it.

PKR Leningrad!

A Moskva-class helicopter carrier that might get overlooked among the "big" and "important" supercarriers, but she's got a lot of heart. At *only* 12,000 tons and about 600 feet long, there's not too much to her, but being built in 1967, she has plenty of life experience with which to guide your relationship. She might not look the part of a Hollywood actress, but her hanger space would be great to start a small family. Not really built for fighting, either to start or end a conflict, and with a respectable if slower speed of 28 knots, she's perfect if you're looing for a quiet life with a partner who will always be there for you, even if she is never the life of the party. Be careful though, she's liable to fall over without warning, so always be on your guard!

TARKR Kirov!

The mighty Kirov! Lead ship of the Kirov-class large missile cruisers, she is the largest surface combatant built since WW2. Weighing in at a whopping 24,000 tons, and over 800 feet long, she's a big gal who knows how to get things going. With 20 long-range anti-ship missiles, and a range of self-defense weapons, she can start and end anything she wants, looking both dangerous and classy at the same time. First built in 1980, she's a newcomer to the dating scene, but her hull-mounter hanger can fit plenty of "kids" and her speed of 32 knots means she can quickly duck and cover if things get a little too spicy. But it's Kirov. She loves it when it's spicy.

RKR Slava!

The first Slava-class missile cruiser, and coming in at 9,300 tons, she's a little thicc compared to most of the other guys/ gals around, but is always in her big sister Kirov's shadow. And no, she isn't self-conscious about it, thank you very much. Her 16 Sandbox anti-ship missiles really aren't easy on the eyes, but she's determined to prove that she can get a party started just as well as her big sister. And with an array of defensive weapons and a 32 knot speed, she can also resolve sticky situations if the need arises. Built in 1982, it'd be hard to find someone younger than her, and her small hanger space means she'll never want a large family.

BPK Nikolayev!

Graceful yet formidable, this Kara-class cruiser is the jack of all trades in the warship dating scene. At 8,200 tons and 560 feet long, her size and length aren't particularly noteworthy, but her 34 knot top speed and single hanger means she can start a family and keep it going through all of life's twists and turns. Like the gorgeous cashier at your local supermarket, she might not look like she'll get into hot water, but her array of weapons, both offensive and defensive, might surprise you! First built in 1971, she's just the right age for anyone who doesn't want someone too inexperienced or too old to be fun.

BPK Otvazhny!

This Kashin-class destroyer is ready for an adventure! First built in 1962, she's been around the block a few times, and isn't here to play games. At 470 feet but only 3,400 tons, she's perfect if you're looking for someone tall and skinny! At 34 knots, she knows where she wants to go in life, and there's no room for kids in a relationship with her. Otherwise, she'll mostly keep to herself, but soon you'll notice how beautiful she is and just how dependable she can be, even if she's quietly supporting her friends and family in the background. A cute little ship that you can always count on, what's not to love?

EM Besstrashny!

Besstrashny means fearless, and let me tell you, this maverick has no fear! One of the Sovremenny-class destroyers, this young thing is coming out swinging, and good luck if you get in her way! At *only* 6,600 tons and just over 500 feet long, she might seem unremarkable, but her deadly Sunburn anti-ship missiles would make even a Kirov or Ticonderoga quake in their stern! Add onto that an instinctive ability to defend herself thanks to a top-of-the-line missile system and a top speed of 32 knots, and you've found a partner who can punch well above her weight. Combined with her flattering twin 5-inch guns and a style like she models for Calvin Klein on the side, she will sweep you off your feet from the moment you are blessed enough to lay eyes on her. First built in 1980, she has plenty of fight still in her, too.

USS Iowa!

When you hear "battleship," you think of Iowa! The first Iowa-class battleship, Iowa is as old-school as can be! First built in 1943, she was considered old when most Cold War warships hadn't even been designed. At 890 feet long and displacing 48,000 tons, calling her a big girl would be an understatement, and her 16-inch guns make a statement both on the streets and in the sheets. Like a true American, she lives a fast life, with no room for a big family, and her upgrades in the 1980s made her quite formidable and ready to get a party started, although she might need help defending herself. While a relic of an older time that has been lost to history, her age and power are still as reliable as when she first ripped apart Japanese ships in World War 2.

USS Ticonderoga!

The lead ship of the Ticonderoga-class cruisers, and named after one of the first Revolutionary War battles, this imposing ship marks the transition between the Cold War and modern era in naval combat. Her boxy superstructure might look like a modern art piece gone wrong, but her advanced combat systems means she can weather all of the storms life throws at you! She doesn't mind being in the spotlight, and her hangar is big enough to support a small family, too. At 9,800 tons and 570 feet long, she's perfectly normal physically, and her 32 knot top speed means she's happy finding a balance in life.

USS Long Beach!

All Hail the Mighty S E A C U B E! USS Long Beach, the only ship of her class built, is a ship that demands attention! Her distinctive bridge superstructure, meant to house experimental radars, is such an eyesore that it comes all the way around to being gorgeous, helped by her 720 feet long, cruiser-style hull. and over 15,000 tons in displacement, she's a little on the bigger side, if you don't mind that. First built in 1961, she's on the older side, and has seen her fair share of the world. Heavily armed and updated throughout her service, she loves both having fun and getting in and out of trouble, and while not interested in having many kids or taking life too fast, she'd be perfect if traveling the world is your joy in life.

USS Spruance!

The first of the Spruance-class destroyers, USS Spruance might look like someone piled a bunch of UPS boxes on top of a ship's hull, but this modest ship has it where it counts. Lightly armed and not possessing many defensive systems, you won't see her getting smashed in a bar or networking with "important" people, but her strength is in doing the unglamorous and overlooked work that others might ignore. At about 8,000 tons and about 560 feet long, her physical characteristics are average, and at 32 knots, she finds a balance in living life. First built in 1975, she's relatively young, and her large hanger and quiet confidence means she's perfect for starting a family.

USS Charles F. Adams!

This Adams-class destroyer is not to be underestimated. While not as intimidating as some other warships, she knows how to defend herself, and it's a mistake to tangle with her unnecessarily. At only 3,200 tons and 430 feet long, she's on the smaller side, but her speed of 33 knots means she's ready to get shit done! She's a little on the shy side, but still ready to deal with any BS that comes her way. With a very balanced design, she's sure to catch your eye, and she's a bit of a lone ranger without any space for helicopters. First built in 1960, she's old enough to see through all the inauthenticity around her, and is the type to get something right the first time and continue on.

USS Knox!

The first ship of the numerous Knox-class frigates (there were 46 built), and a quiet individual. She wasn't really built for an exciting life, so don't expect to find her in the club or cornering the stock market. Having only basic defenses, she was built to hunt submarines in the Atlantic, so she's more of a prowler than a brawler. At 4,000 tons and 400 feet long, she's understandably small, and not likely to wow you at first sight. That said, moving at a stately 27 knots and with a decently sized aircraft hangar, she's an ideal partner for anyone who cares more about enjoying life and building a small family than making waves.

JS Haruna!

One of the two Haruna-class, she'll tell you she's a "helicopter destroyer," but she's probably just saying that to exploit some kind of legal loophole. Dedicated to carrying up to three helicopters, she's very much willing to start a big family if that's what you're looking for, although her imposing superstructure means she might not win many beauty contests. She's probably the kind of ship who's going to hide from the spotlight as much as possible, and she's more likely to de-escalate a situation rather than escalate it. At 5,000 tons and 500 feet long, she's perfectly normal, and clocking in at 31 knots, is ready to take life as it comes while still finding time to value friends and family. Be sure to find the best sushi spots around, though!

JDS Tachikaze!

First of the Japanese Tachikaze-class guided missile destroyers, the Tachikaze may not seem like much, but she's a force to be reckoned with in the right situations. Built in 1976, she's relatively new to the scene, and is full of energy! With advanced computers and sensor systems, it's easy for her to spot trouble, and she has a good chance of making it out unscathed. She generally prefers American styles, but might be ashamed to admit it out of fear of embarrassing her Japanese heritage. At 32 knots, she's ready to live life to the fullest, but has time for the quieter things, evidenced by her name, which translates to "standing wind." She's 3,800 tons and 470 feet long, which means she's tall and skinny, and she's intelligently laid out, giving a confident appearance. She knows what she's doing, and you won't forget that.

PLAN Nanjing!

One of the many Luda-class destroyers of the People's Liberation Army Navy (And yes that is the name of the Chinese Navy), it might be easy to mix her up with one of her many sisters and half-sisters. Big mistake! She's well armed with the best of, well, 1950's missile technology, but that can still ruin your day if you're not careful! Her manual air-defense artillery is old by most standards, but with a speed of 38 knots, she can at least get out of trouble and move on to the next big thing quickly! She's not the type at all to stop and smell the roses, so you best be ready! She's only 430 feet long and displaces about 3,600 tons, so she's perfect to be the little spoon, if you can get her to relax for five minutes. Very unique, she has her charm once you get a good look at her. Don't expect a large family though, since her lack of a hanger and fast pace means she has much bigger goals to aim for.

USS Los Angeles!

The apex predator of the Cold War-era undersea environment, USS Los Angeles, first of her class of dozens (62 to be exact!) is not someone to be trifled with. Deadly quiet, and packing some of the most advanced submarine weapons in the world, she's perfect for making trouble. Prove you're adept at jump-scaring others in random circumstances, and you'll win a place in her heart. She might start out stealthy and unseen, but when she wants to, she can make quite the appearance. Displacing 6,000 tons and measuring 360 feet long, she might be a little big for her size, but her smooth, bullet-like hull makes you notice her, and her sail and dive planes make her look formidable. First built in 1976, and with a speed of over 33 knots, she's a young American monster, ready to make her mark on the strange world under the ocean's surface.

B-401 Novisibirsk!

One of the many Kilo-class submarines, B-401 is a fine example of Soviet and Russian engineering. First built in 1980, she's just now entering the wider world, and is ready to draw your attention. In the right circumstances, she can be the life of the party, even if she might need help getting out of sticky situations. At 2,300 tons and 240 feet long, she can't really see things from a birds-eye view, and her unique teardrop hull might be more curious than flattering. With the older diesel-electric engines, she might be the sneakiest person around, although with a speed of only 20 knots, don't expect her to dream too big when it comes to setting life goals together. Being a submarine, there isn't much room at all, much less for a family, but when it comes to stealth, you have the right ship.
